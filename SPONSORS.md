# 💚 Sponsors

This page is the public record of who supports my open-source work and what that support paid for.

Two ways to sponsor, both through [GitHub Sponsors](https://github.com/sponsors/Borda):

- **Say thank you.** Small monthly tiers keep the projects I own maintained and released.
- **Buy a service.** Workshops, CI and governance reviews, AI-assisted review setup, and priority work in my projects are priced tiers — paid through GitHub instead of an invoice. The catalogue with prices is in [SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md).

**Two prices, one rule.** The *OSS price* applies when the result is public: work on repositories under an OSI-approved licence, or an engagement where you agree to a public reference. The *standard price* applies to private or proprietary code and to anything under NDA. A public reference means the four items listed at the bottom of this page — nothing more, and nothing published without your approval.

## What sponsorship funded

The moving numbers, updated monthly: GPU minutes bought through [affordable-GPU-CI](https://github.com/Borda/affordable-GPU-CI), releases shipped, and issues closed across the projects sponsorship covers — [AI-Rig](https://github.com/Borda/AI-Rig), [affordable-GPU-CI](https://github.com/Borda/affordable-GPU-CI), [fuse-augmentations](https://github.com/Borda/fuse-augmentations), [vision-delivery](https://github.com/Borda/vision-delivery), [pyDeprecate](https://github.com/Borda/pyDeprecate). Two line items eat the money. **GPU time** — experimenting, debugging and profiling on real NVIDIA hardware, plus the throughput benchmarks behind [fuse-augmentations](https://github.com/Borda/fuse-augmentations), where a transform is only worth shipping if it is measurably faster: notebook credits (Colab and friends) for prototyping, serverless GPU minutes for the automated runs. **Agent credits** — the model spend behind [AI-Rig](https://github.com/Borda/AI-Rig): running review and release agents against real repositories, headless runs in CI, and re-testing the plugin suite whenever a model or harness changes. Neither is free, and both are what keep the tooling honest rather than theoretical.

## What a public reference means

When you take the OSS price, four things become part of delivery — agreed before work starts, collected within five business days of handover:

1. **Public sponsorship** — the sponsorship toggle set to public, so your logo appears in the grid on the Sponsors profile.
2. **Logo and link** — an SVG or PNG plus the URL it should point to, shown on this page and, for priority-work sponsors and larger engagements, in the README of one project you pick. One project, not all of them.
3. **A quotable sentence or two** — with your name and role, published only after you approve the exact text.
4. **A dated log line** — naming the deliverable and its scale, written by me, no amounts and no client code.

A short write-up of the work may follow: no client code, no internal details, and you approve the text before it goes out. The `reference: yes / no` field is filled in the scope email before any work starts. No reply means private, and private means the standard price.
